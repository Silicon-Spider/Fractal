# Ideas pass, 27 November 2026: Stockfish's main branch and the other engines

On 27 November 2026 Fergus asked for a look at Stockfish 19, Stockfish's main branch since that release, and the other engines in development, for ideas Fractal should test. This note is the result. It is written for the next revision of `docs/SPEC.md` and `docs/DECISIONS.md` and changes neither. Ideas only, no code [NF12]: each idea is stated in this note's own words, with the commit it came from.

It was prepared in Fergus's review session of that day, outside the repository, and filed on 28 November. Nothing in it has been run against Fractal's code or data, and the coding agent has not checked it.

The pass covers inputs, the network, data and training. Search ideas wait for a second pass before part c; the few that surfaced are noted.

## Summary

- **Stockfish 19 was already in spec §13.3; its main branch since was not.** Main has 129 commits since the release of 5 September and four net designs, SFNNv17 on 30 September to SFNNv20 on 9 November. Only the first, the removal of the piece-value path, is in the spec.
- **Seven arms are proposed, NX28 to NX34, and two arms gain a note, NX13 and NX14.** Four are screens on stored data, two wait for G4 and one is a match. None changes v1.
- **The strongest case in 5D is a second key for the output stacks (NX28).** It costs nothing at evaluation, and Fractal's one key leaves most human positions in two stacks.
- **One note for part b needs no test:** the head's first layer can skip empty roles, which are whole blocks of zeros in most positions.
- **One line of §13.3 is wrong** and is corrected below.
- **Most of what moved does not bear on Fractal.** About a quarter of Stockfish's commits since the release tune search constants for chess. Lc0, KataGo, YaneuraOu, dlshogi and bullet changed nothing a decision here rests on.
- **Three rulings are needed.** One is on the record format, which ND1 commits.

## Correction to §13.3

The "Stockfish 18–19" row ends: "Stockfish 19 dropped king victims and pawn pairs (79,856 → 59,808 features)". Pawn pairs were added, not dropped. Between the two releases the threat set dropped threats to kings and from kings (SFNNv11 and v12, at the start of February) and, on 20 July, its pawn-on-pawn rows, because the new pawn-pair family covers them.

Proposed wording: "Between 18 and 19 the threat set dropped threats to and from kings and, once a separate pawn-pair family covered them, its pawn-on-pawn rows (79,856 → 59,808 features)." The two counts are the spec's own and were not rechecked.

## The proposed arms

Each is one change against v1 [P5], in §15.1's two kinds. Evidence is from chess unless a line says otherwise; none of it is from 5D.

**NX28, a second key for the output stacks.** v1 picks one of five stacks by the active-timeline count [NN7]. In human play that key barely splits the data: 85% of distinct turn starts have under 50 boards, where the mean is 1.4 active timelines (§8), so most positions land in the first two stacks. A second key splits them by what is on the boards.

- **Forms to screen:** (a) which sides have a queen on a head board, four states; (b) a three-way class of pieces per head board, its edges cut from gen-1 data; (c) either key selecting only the head's later layers.
- **Training** keeps NN7's shared part and its fallback for starved classes. The material path's columns follow the stacks [NN13].
- **Cost.** None at evaluation, since a key selects weights. In the file a full stack is about 95,000 8-bit weights; form (c) adds about a thousand per class.
- **Spec text.** §7.5 names the stack selector as the one use of a global count. If the arm is adopted, it names the second key beside the first.

**NX14, second form.** King buckets, also split by whether the enemy has a queen on the board. A board's piece accumulator is rebuilt from scratch when its bucket changes, at most 64 rows, so the form fits §8.2. The table is the cost: sixteen buckets take the gen-1 piece table from 0.6 MB to 9.6 MB at 16 bits, against a 3.4 MB net file.

**NX29, piece rows in 8 bits.** It halves the piece table and is worth running only with NX14. Fractal's relation rows are already 8-bit [NN11]. The exporter's bound proof and NM4's checksums apply unchanged. NN11 is committed, so adopting the arm would take a ruling.

**NX30, facing pawns as relation rows.** A pawn whose forward step is blocked by a pawn of either colour, indexed like any other relation. The rows passed Stockfish's gain tests at both time controls in April. Whether pawn structure matters in 5D is unknown, so it runs last.

**NX31, search-tree samples.** Positions per result are the scarce input: a human game has about 39 turns (§9.5). The search visits many more turn starts than the game plays, and those on its principal variations carry exact scores.

- **What is stored.** For a searched position, a few turn starts from the principal variations of its search, each as the line of turns that reaches it, with its own score, depth and flags. That is still games and positions, never features [NF7].
- **How it trains.** On its score alone, as a capped game's positions already do (§9.2). The flags let the quiet filter apply or not at load [ND6].
- **When.** Gen 1's scores come from the material-only bootstrap, so its samples would teach little. The arm runs at G4 on gen 2's data. The record format should reserve room for the list now (ruling 2).
- **Evidence.** Thin. Stockfish's trainer accepts such samples since 15 November, and its note gives no result.

**NX32, decided positions thinned at load.** A position the search scores beyond ±2.5 K, a win probability of about 92%, is kept three times in ten. It needs no new data. Games in 5D almost always end in mate (§13.2), so the decided share may be large; G2's data report should state it.

**NX33, a wider teacher behind the scores.** A net several times wider than the playing net, trained on the same window, re-searches the stored positions, and the playing net trains on those scores.

- **Within P6 as written:** the label is still a search score of Fractal's own, from Fractal's own net and data [NF5], and the teacher never ships [NF2]. A teacher's bare evaluation as a label would need a ruling.
- **When.** At G4, beside ND9's rescoring, which it is a form of. Kairos's deep relabelling of hard positions did not gain (§13.2); a stronger evaluator behind the search is the untested half.
- **Cost.** A second training run on a desktop CPU [NL4]. G2's trainer speed says whether that is hours or days.

**NX34, quiet order from the relation scan.** The scan has already run when the generator is built (§6.1), and it knows which of the mover's pieces an enemy relation reaches. A quiet move of such a piece, when the attacker's ordering value is lower or no own relation defends it, scores above history in the quiet band [NT4]. Where the static eval came from the hash table the scan has not run, and the order falls back to history. It is an ordering rule and never an evaluation term.

**The note on NX13.** With the attacker's square in the index, a same-board row whose attacker's capture geometry lies inside its victim's repeats the reverse row and can be dropped. The rules engine's own probe lists the kind pairs. In v1's index the two rows are not duplicates: each holds a different piece's square.

**Order.** Screens: NX28 after NX19; NX29 after NX14; NX32 after NX5; NX30 last. NX31 and NX33 sit outside the screen order, at G4. Matches: NX34 after NX18.

## Notes for part b

- **Empty roles are zero blocks.** A role with no board gives zeros [NN5]. In a one-timeline position at most 416 of the 1,472 inputs per view can be non-zero: the mandatory role's sum and max, and the two past roles. The first head layer can run over populated roles only, and within them over non-zero inputs. The result is exact, so NM4's checksums don't move. It shows in NM6's stage split and counts toward NN12.
- **Row traffic is small in v1.** About 21.5 relation rows per position are added at each evaluation (§7.3); the rest are cached with their boards. Stockfish's paired widening of 8-bit rows (17 October; 5–11% of its speed on x86 by its own measure) and its row prefetches (7 February and 14 November; the later one gained on some builds and lost on others) pay where row adds dominate. Neither is proposed.
- **G2's data report gains three lines:** positions per NX28 class, the share of decided positions, and the share of zero inputs to the head by stack.
- **Part c states how many key bits a probe verifies** [NA2], and the false-hit rate that implies. A false hit here could carry a proven bit.

## Seen and not taken

- **Pawn pairs** (Stockfish, SFNNv16). A large family with no 5D evidence behind it; in Stockfish it failed the short test and passed the long ones, at about 3.5% slower. If NX30 passes its screen, pairs are its next form.
- **One accumulator for pieces and relations** (Stockfish, 10 June; PlentyChess, 5 July). It halved their accumulator traffic. Here it would put per-evaluation relations in front of the cached board embedding and undo §8.2's cache. NX24 already tests mixing the parts.
- **Stockfish's reworked skip connections** (SFNNv15, 3 July). Its notes do not describe them, and no source was read. NX15 and NX24 already vary the head.
- **Arithmetic slider attacks in place of magic tables** (Stockfish, 21 November). The copied engine has no magic tables to retire.
- **Repetition and cuckoo-table work** (Stockfish, 14 and 20 November). 5D has no repetition rule (R5).
- **About 32 search-tuning commits** on Stockfish's main: constants for null move, reductions, razoring, histories and time. They are fitted to chess at a test volume Fractal does not have.
- **Correction history split by queen presence** (Stockfish, 26 November). NX7 has no result yet to refine.
- **dlshogi's mate-solver speed work** (about 20 commits since August). NX21 stands as written.
- **KataGo's floored weight decay and revived dead parameters** (13 September), **YaneuraOu's option for 16-bit dense weights** (10 September), **Lc0's GPU back ends** and **bullet's plumbing.** Nothing a decision here rests on.
- **"Lambda jitter"** in Stockfish's trainer (13 May). Its note does not say what it varies. Not examined.

## Rulings needed

1. **The arms.** Add NX28 to NX34 and the notes on NX13 and NX14, as above. **Recommended:** yes. An arm costs nothing until it runs, and §15.1's order decides that.
2. **Room for tree samples in the record.** ND1 is committed and committed-class, and the format gets expensive to change once gen-1 data exists. (a) Reserve an optional list of tree samples per searched position in the format now, and leave it empty in gen 1. (b) Leave the format alone, and extend it if NX31 is ever run. **Recommended:** (a). It costs a field, and whether to fill it stays NX31's question at G4.
3. **The correction to §13.3.** **Recommended:** yes.
